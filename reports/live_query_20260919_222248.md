**Question:** What accuracy did the VGG16 and CNN models achieve for lung cancer CT scan classification, and which performed better?

## Answer

In the comparative study evaluating convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures for three-class lung cancer CT scan classification (Normal, Benign, Malignant), the models achieved the following test accuracies:

* **VGG16:** **98.18%** [AI-Powered Lung Cancer Detection- Assessing VGG16 and CNN Architectures for CT Scan Image Classification.pdf, p.25]
* **MobileNetV2:** **93.64%** (and a training accuracy of 97.15%) [AI-Powered Lung Cancer Detection- Assessing VGG16 and CNN Architectures for CT Scan Image Classification.pdf, p.13, p.25]
* **ResNet50:** **93.33%** [AI-Powered Lung Cancer Detection- Assessing VGG16 and CNN Architectures for CT Scan Image Classification.pdf, p.25]
* **InceptionV3:** **88.79%** [AI-Powered Lung Cancer Detection- Assessing VGG16 and CNN Architectures for CT Scan Image Classification.pdf, p.25]

### Which performed better:
**VGG16 performed the best**, achieving the highest overall test accuracy (98.18%), superior generalization, and the lowest misclassification rate across categories, outperforming the other CNN architectures [AI-Powered Lung Cancer Detection- Assessing VGG16 and CNN Architectures for CT Scan Image Classification.pdf, p.13, p.25]. This also aligns with prior literature cited in the paper confirming that deeper pre-trained networks like VGG16 consistently outperform traditional CNN models in lung cancer classification [AI-Powered Lung Cancer Detection- Assessing VGG16 and CNN Architectures for CT Scan Image Classification.pdf, p.3, p.13].
